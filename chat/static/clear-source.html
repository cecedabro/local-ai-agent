How Clear Source works

Clear Source · Dewdrop's medical research mode

Answers only from sources it found and read, and every quote checked word for word.

A language model on its own mixes what it read with what it remembers. Clear Source doesn't trust the model to be strict: the app runs the searches, lets the model read one source at a time, keeps only passages that really appear in the source, and checks the finished answer again. This page follows one question through every step.

Example question“Wat is de eerste keus behandeling bij een blaasontsteking bij vrouwen?”

  1. 0

    Your question

    the app

    Your message is stored first, then the answer runs as a task on the server. If your phone locks or you switch apps, it keeps going and the page picks it up again when you come back.

    Files you attached

    Read first and treated as sources, before anything is searched.

    Answer language

    Decided by the app from your words: Dutch words like wat, bij, werkt mean a Dutch answer; otherwise English. The model is told so explicitly.

  2. 1

    Understand: make a search plan

    model call

    One model call turns the question into a short summary and at most 7 searches. The prompt (PLAN_PROMPT) asks for:

    • PubMed: 2 to 4 searches in English, from specific to broad, each with up to 4 official MeSH headings.
    • Websites: the 1 to 3 trusted sites that fit best. A medicine question always includes the Farmacotherapeutisch Kompas; children's doses the Kinderformularium; pregnancy NVOG; when Dutch sites may be silent NICE, WHO or CDC. Dutch sites are searched in Dutch, English sites in English.

    In

    Wat is de eerste keus behandeling bij een
blaasontsteking bij vrouwen?

    Out (JSON)

    {"about": "Eerste keus bij een ongecompliceerde
           blaasontsteking bij vrouwen.",
 "searches": [
  {"source": "site:richtlijnendatabase.nl",
   "query": "blaasontsteking behandeling eerste keus"},
  {"source": "site:farmacotherapeutischkompas.nl",
   "query": "nitrofurantoïne cystitis"},
  {"source": "pubmed",
   "query": "cystitis women first line treatment",
   "mesh": ["Cystitis", "Anti-Bacterial Agents"]}]}

    You see "Understanding the question", with the summary and the search terms as chips.

  3. 2

    Search: the app runs the plan

    the app

    No model here: the app does the searching, so it can't invent results. Guideline sites go first, so PubMed can't crowd them out.

    PubMed

    1. The MeSH headings are checked against PubMed's MeSH database; made-up ones are dropped.
    2. The search runs with the profile's filters (study types, years, humans, languages):
    (("Cystitis"[MeSH Terms] AND "Anti-Bacterial Agents"[MeSH Terms])
 OR (cystitis women first line treatment))
AND (systematic[sb] OR meta-analysis[pt]
     OR randomized controlled trial[pt]
     OR (guideline[pt] OR practice guideline[pt]))
AND "last 10 years"[dp] AND humans[mh]
    1. Fewer than 3 hits: the same words once more without filters and MeSH ("broader").
    2. At most ~3 requests a second to PubMed, retried when it says "too many requests".

    Trusted websites

    1. A site-restricted search through the app's own search engine (SearXNG).
    2. The top 2 pages are opened and turned into text (at most 8 000 characters each).
    3. PubMed Central articles are read in full text.
    4. Pages behind a bot check ("Just a moment…") are refused, never bypassed. That's why NHG isn't a source.

    Open web

    Off by default for Clear Source: open web results let advocacy sites in.

    You see one row per search, with what it found and a link to the exact search.

  4. 3

    Read: one source at a time

    model call per source

    Up to 20 candidate sources. For each one, a separate model call (EXTRACT_PROMPT):

    “Copy, word for word, up to 4 passages of the source that help answer the question: whole sentences, exactly as written, in the source's own language; change nothing.”

    The app checks every passage

    A passage counts only if it is word for word in that source. A near miss (a changed space, quote mark or a lost "²") is swapped for the source's exact words; a passage cut off mid-word runs on to the end of its sentence. A title is never a passage. Whatever is kept is word for word in the source.

    Result per source

    2 quotes useful, nothing on this question, quotes didn't match the text, off topic, skipped (a broad PubMed hit that shares no search word), or could not read.

    Round 2. Fewer than 3 useful sources? One more model call (FOLLOWUP_PROMPT) plans new searches with other words (synonyms, Dutch and English, broader terms, other sites), and steps 2 and 3 run once more.

    You see each source appear with its result: "1 quote", "nothing on this question".

  5. 4

    Write: only from the kept passages

    model call, thinks first

    The useful sources (at most 10) are put in order: guidelines first, then meta-analyses and reviews, then trials, then the rest. Only their kept passages go to the model, not the whole pages. Clear Source always lets the model reason before it writes.

    The rules (RULES)

    • Answer only from the numbered passages, never from memory.
    • Fixed headings: Kort antwoord · Nederlandse richtlijnen · Onderzoek · Dosering en bijzondere groepen · Sterkte van het bewijs.
    • Build each part from quotes: > "exact words" [2], explained below in plain words.
    • Each quote once; the short answer has no quotes.
    • Quotes stay in the source's language; the rest in the question's language.
    • Nothing useful found? The app says so itself, without asking the model.

    Out

    ## Kort antwoord
De eerste keus is nitrofurantoïne, vijf dagen [1].

## Nederlandse richtlijnen
> "Behandel een ongecompliceerde cystitis bij
   niet-zwangere vrouwen met nitrofurantoïne." [1]

De richtlijn noemt nitrofurantoïne als eerste
middel; de dosering staat in het Kompas [2].

    You see the text being written; the numbers are clickable straight away.

  6. 5

    Check: compare the answer with the sources again

    the app

    The finished answer goes through the checker (verify.py), which knows nothing about the model:

    ✓

    The quote is found word for word in the source it cites.

    removed

    Before that, a quote that is in none of its sources is taken out of the answer (a near miss is first swapped for the source's exact words); a note says how many.

    ⚠ Bronnen verschillen

    When two sources disagree, the answer says so in a box, and follows the guideline.

    1 sentence has no source

    A claim without a [n].

    Then the answer is stored with its sources, the research trail and the check, so it looks the same when you open it later. Tap a number or a checked quote to open its source.

What travels to your screen

The answer arrives as a stream of small events. When the connection drops, the server keeps them, and the page replays them when it comes back.

  1. phasewhat it's doing now: the label and the drop's animation
  2. trailone research step: a plan, a search, a source read
  3. thinkingthe model's reasoning while it writes
  4. sourcesthe sources it will cite, so the [n] work while writing
  5. deltaa piece of the answer's text
  6. verifythe checker's report
  7. donestored: message id, sources, trail, speed

Good to know

  • Time. About 10 model calls: 1 to 3 minutes with a free GPU, longer while the coding agent uses the model.
  • Search decides the answer. When PubMed's best matches are off-topic, most sources are rightly rejected and the answer is short, or "no reliable evidence found".
  • Abstracts. PubMed gives abstracts; only PubMed Central articles are read in full.
  • The checker is literal. A correct paraphrase without quote marks and a number counts as "no source".
  • Sources. PubMed, Richtlijnendatabase, RIVM (LCI), JGZ, Palliaweb, NVOG, NICE, WHO, CDC, Thuisarts, Farmacotherapeutisch Kompas, Kinderformularium, Lareb, Geneesmiddelenbulletin, Apotheek.nl and EMA. Change them on the Profiles page.
